Low-set eyebrows sit closer to the eyes, reducing the vertical distance between the brow ridge and the upper eyelid. This positioning creates a more intense, grounded facial expression and is often associated with a stronger, more assertive appearance. If your brows sit high on your forehead, you may want to bring them down visually or structurally. There are several ways to do this, ranging from simple grooming adjustments to more involved cosmetic procedures.

This guide walks through all of them, from the most accessible to the most committed, so you can decide what fits your goals and situation.

## What Are Low Set Eyebrows?

Brow position is measured relative to the superior orbital rim, which is the bony ridge just above your eye socket. When the eyebrows sit close to this ridge, they are considered low-set. When they arc high above it, they are considered high-set.

Low-set brows tend to:

- Reduce the visible forehead height
- Create a heavier, more dominant brow expression
- Give the face a more serious or intense resting look
- Make the eyes appear more deep-set

High-set brows, by contrast, open up the upper face and can give a more surprised or youthful expression. Neither is objectively better. The right look depends on your bone structure, face shape, and personal aesthetic goals.

## Why People Want Lower Eyebrows

The desire for lower brows is largely aesthetic. Certain facial archetypes associated with masculinity and intensity, often described in terms of facial harmony or the PSL framework, include a brow that sits lower and more horizontally over the eye socket. This creates what many call a "hooded" or "hunter eye" appearance, where the upper eyelid is partially covered by the brow or lid fold.

Beyond aesthetic trends, some people simply feel their naturally high brows make their face look more open or anxious than they feel on the inside. Lowering the brows, even slightly, can bring the face into better harmony with their desired look.

## Grooming Approaches: Shaping to Create the Illusion

Before committing to anything more permanent, grooming is the most sensible starting point. You cannot actually move the hair follicles downward with grooming, but you can shape the brow to *read* as lower on the face.

### Remove Hair from the Top, Not the Bottom

Most people who groom their brows pluck or wax the underside. If you want a lower-set appearance, do the opposite: clean up the top edge of the brow instead. Removing hair from the upper border of the brow brings the visual weight of the arch downward without physically moving anything.

Do not over-thin the brow at the top. The goal is a subtle taper, not a sharp line.

### Reduce the Arch Height

A high arch pulls the brow upward visually. If your brows have a pronounced peak, flattening the arch by trimming or filling it in more horizontally will lower its perceived position. A straighter, more horizontal brow reads as lower-set than an arched one.

To flatten:

1. Identify the highest point of your natural arch.
2. Remove a few hairs just at the peak to bring the arch down.
3. When filling in, use horizontal strokes to create a flatter line rather than following the upward curve.

### Grow Out the Lower Edge

If you have been plucking the underside of your brow heavily, stop. Letting the lower edge grow in will naturally bring the brow shape downward. This takes patience, often four to eight weeks of growth, but it is one of the more effective grooming changes you can make.

## Makeup Techniques for Lower-Looking Brows

Makeup gives you the most flexibility because it is reversible and adjustable.

### Concealer to Raise the Apparent Forehead

One counterintuitive trick: apply a slightly lighter concealer or highlighter just above the brow, extending toward the hairline. This brightens the forehead area and draws the eye upward, which paradoxically can make the brow appear to sit lower relative to the eye below it. This works best as part of a broader contouring routine.

### Fill the Brow Lower

When filling in your brows with a pencil or powder:

- Start the fill on the lower edge of the brow shape rather than the center.
- Use light, feathery strokes to add density below the natural brow line.
- Keep the top edge sharp and clean.

This shifts the visual mass of the brow downward by a few millimeters, which can be enough to noticeably change the way the brow sits on the face.

### Contour the Forehead

A larger forehead emphasizes high brow placement. Contouring the upper forehead with a matte bronzer or contour powder just below the hairline compresses the apparent forehead height, making the brows look lower by comparison.

## Hairstyling and Hairline Considerations

Your hairline affects how high your forehead looks, and therefore how high your brows appear. A hairline that sits high on the skull creates more vertical space, which makes brows look higher-set even if they are not.

Bangs or fringe, especially straight-cut styles that sit just above the brow, are a direct way to reduce visible forehead space and make the brows appear closer to the center of the face rather than near the top.

For men, textured or forward-swept styles that bring hair over part of the forehead can work similarly.

## Cosmetic Procedures: More Permanent Options

If grooming and makeup do not get you where you want to be, there are cosmetic options worth knowing about. These range from minimally invasive to surgical.

**Important: Always consult a qualified, board-certified medical professional before considering any cosmetic procedure. The information below is educational only and does not constitute medical advice.**

### Botox Brow Depression

Botox can be used to lower the brow position by relaxing the frontalis muscle, the broad muscle across the forehead that pulls the brows upward. When this muscle is partially relaxed, the brow descends slightly. Some practitioners target specific points to lower the lateral or medial brow depending on the look desired.

This is a nuanced procedure. Poorly placed injections can cause brow asymmetry or a heavy, drooping appearance that looks unnatural. Results last approximately three to four months. Some users report a noticeable lowering of about three to five millimeters with well-placed injections.

### Brow Lowering Surgery (Forehead Reduction or Brow Descent)

For a permanent change in brow position, brow descent or forehead reduction surgery is the most direct option. In forehead reduction, a surgeon removes a strip of skin at the hairline, which pulls the entire scalp, including the brows, downward. In a brow descent procedure, the brows themselves are repositioned surgically.

These are significant procedures with real recovery times, risks, and costs. They are generally reserved for people who have exhausted non-surgical options and have a clear, stable aesthetic goal. Recovery can take several weeks, and results are permanent.

Again, consult a board-certified plastic surgeon or facial plastic surgeon if you are seriously considering this route. ## Building a Realistic Plan

Here is a practical sequence for approaching this goal:

1. **Assess your current brow position honestly.** Take a neutral-expression photo in even lighting and look at the distance between your brow and your upper eyelid. Is the gap large or small? Is the arch high or flat?
2. **Start with grooming changes.** Adjust your arch shape, let the lower edge grow, and clean up the top. Give it six to eight weeks before judging results.
3. **Add makeup techniques if needed.** Fill the brow lower, flatten the arch with your pencil, and experiment with forehead contouring.
4. **Consider hairline and hairstyle adjustments.** Sometimes this alone does more than any brow-specific change.
5. **If you want more change than non-invasive methods allow, consult a professional.** Botox is the lowest commitment cosmetic option and is reversible. Surgery is permanent and should be considered carefully.

Results from grooming and makeup are immediate and reversible. Botox effects are visible within one to two weeks and fade. Surgical results are long-term and require thorough planning.

## Managing Expectations

It is worth being direct about what each approach can actually achieve. Grooming and makeup can create the *appearance* of a lower brow but do not move the follicle or the bone. The underlying anatomy stays the same. For many people, a well-executed grooming and makeup approach is enough to get the look they want in photos and everyday life.

Cosmetic procedures offer more structural change but come with cost, risk, and recovery. A few millimeters of brow lowering may not be visible to anyone but you, or it may significantly change the character of your face. It depends heavily on your starting point.

The most useful thing you can do before making any decision is to get a clear, objective view of your face, including brow height, forehead proportion, and eye area depth, so your goals are grounded in your actual anatomy rather than a general idea of what you want to look like.
