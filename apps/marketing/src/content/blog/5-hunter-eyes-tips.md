Search "how to get hunter eyes" and you'll find two kinds of answers. One kind promises that exercises, routines or posture tricks will reshape your eyes. The other kind is less exciting and more useful. Most of the look comes from bone and eyelid anatomy you were born with, and a smaller part comes from how you present it.

This guide takes the second approach. You'll learn what the term actually describes, which parts are fixed, and five practical tips for making the most of the eye area you have. No miracle claims and no fake before-and-after photos.

## What "hunter eyes" actually means

"Hunter eyes" is slang, not a medical term. It comes from looksmaxxing forums, and [it has no clinical definition](https://trymaxxing.com/blogs/news/hunter-eyes). The terms "hunter eyes" and "prey eyes" are [internet coinages rather than anatomical terminology](https://softmaxx.io/blog/hunter-eyes-vs-prey-eyes), but they point to real, describable features.

In community usage, the label is [shorthand for a cluster of traits](https://thefacereport.com/guides/hunter-eyes):

- **Positive canthal tilt.** The outer corner of the eye sits higher than the inner corner. In anatomical writing, canthal tilt is described as [the inclination of the eye's horizontal axis between the inner corner (endocanthion) and the outer corner (exocanthion)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4765746/).
- **Low upper-eyelid exposure.** Little of the upper lid shows between the lashes and the crease when your eyes are relaxed.
- **A straighter lower lid.** The bottom edge of the eye opening runs flatter instead of curving deeply.
- **Deep-set eyes.** The eyes sit back under the brow, so the brow ridge frames and shades them.

The contrast term is "prey eyes." These are [rounder and more open, with high lid exposure and a neutral or slightly negative tilt](https://softmaxx.io/blog/hunter-eyes).

One point gets lost in forum threads. Canthal tilt is the most cited trait, but [there is no agreed scientific threshold at which positive tilt becomes "hunter eyes"](https://thefacereport.com/guides/hunter-eyes). If someone gives you an exact degree number as the cutoff, they are stating an opinion, not a measured standard.

## What you can't change, and what you can

Before picking tips, sort the traits into two groups. This one step saves months of chasing things that won't move.

### Largely fixed

- **Orbital depth, eye shape and the bony orbital rim.** These are skeletal. [In an adult they do not change without surgery, and no exercise, device or routine alters them](https://www.looksmeter.com/hunter-eyes).
- **Overall eye shape.** [Eye shape is largely determined by genetics](https://www.eyeplasticsmn.com/can-eye-shape-change-by-itself/).
- **Canthal tilt.** This is set by eyelid and bone anatomy. You can change how it *appears* in a photo, but not the anatomy itself.

### Changes over time

- **Upper-lid exposure** depends on brow ridge, orbit shape and soft tissue. It [also changes with age as eyelid skin loses elasticity](https://softmaxx.io/blog/hunter-eyes-vs-prey-eyes).
- **Brow position** also shifts. [As we age, brows tend to sag and weigh down the upper eyelids](https://www.eyeplasticsmn.com/can-eye-shape-change-by-itself/). This is aging, not a deeper orbit, and it isn't a goal to aim for.

### Within your control

- How you groom and shape your brows
- How you light, angle and frame photos of your face
- Your expression, especially whether you habitually widen your eyes

One source lists the main controls on lid exposure as [orbital depth, fat pad distribution, brow positioning and genetics](https://www.stylementor.fashion/2026/02/eye-area-aesthetics-hunter-eyes-guide.html). Of those four, brow positioning is the one you can influence day to day, through grooming and expression. That is why the tips below focus there.

## 5 realistic tips for a hunter-eye look

These are editorial styling suggestions, not measured or clinical results. They change presentation, not anatomy.

### 1. Break the look into its parts before chasing it

"Hunter eyes" bundles four separate traits under one label. Few people have all four strongly, and plenty of people have one or two. Instead of asking "do I have hunter eyes?", ask more specific questions:

1. Does my brow sit fairly low and close to my eye?
2. How much upper lid shows when my face is relaxed?
3. Do my outer eye corners look upward, level or downward?
4. Do my eyes sit back under the brow or level with it?

This matters because each trait responds differently. Brow grooming affects how the brow line looks. Lighting affects how deep-set the eyes appear. Tilt and orbital depth don't respond to either. Knowing which trait you're working on stops you from expecting a brow trim to fix your canthal tilt.

### 2. Check your eye area under fair conditions

Most self-assessments go wrong at the photo stage. Small changes in camera position change what your eyes appear to do:

- **Camera roll.** If the phone is rotated even slightly, a level line through your eye corners will look tilted.
- **Head pose.** Tilting your chin up or down, or turning your head, changes how the inner and outer corners line up in the image.
- **Lens distance.** A phone held very close distorts the features nearest the lens.

For a fair check, use a straight-on photo at eye level, at arm's length or more, with your head level, your face relaxed and even light. Take a few shots and compare them. If the results disagree, treat the tilt as uncertain.

### 3. Groom your brows toward a lower, straighter line

Your brows are the most adjustable part of the eye-area frame. Brow lifting moves things in the opposite direction from the hunter look: [a more lifted brow is what produces a more open eye area](https://drturner.com.au/blogs/blepharoplasty-eye-shape/). So if you want a more hooded, focused look, avoid grooming that raises or sharply arches the brow.

Editorial suggestions:

- **Clean up, don't reshape.** Remove stray hairs between the brows and well below the natural brow line. Leave the bottom edge mostly intact, since over-plucking it pushes the visible brow line upward.
- **Keep the body full.** A dense brow sitting close to the eye reads as a stronger frame than a thin, high one.
- **Flatten a heavy arch carefully.** If you have a peaked arch, let hairs at the peak grow in rather than plucking around it. Results depend on your natural growth.
- **Trim long hairs, don't remove them.** Brush brow hairs upward and trim only the ends that extend past the line. This keeps the shape tidy without thinning it.

If you prefer a softer or more arched brow, which is common in feminine styling, that's a valid choice. The hunter look is one aesthetic, not a requirement, and you don't need to adopt it.

### 4. Control lighting, angle and expression in photos

Much of what people call "hunter eyes" in photos is shadow. Deep-set eyes look deep because the brow ridge shades the upper lid. Lighting can emphasize or flatten that effect without changing anything about your face.

Editorial suggestions for photos:

- **Light from slightly above.** Light from above and slightly in front casts a soft shadow under the brow. Flat, direct front light, like an on-camera flash, removes that shadow and makes the eye area look more open.
- **Keep the camera at eye level.** Shooting from far below exaggerates lid exposure and nostrils. Shooting from far above changes the brow-to-eye relationship in the other direction. Eye level is the most honest default.
- **Relax your eyes.** Many people widen their eyes when a camera points at them, which shows more upper lid and sclera (the white of the eye). Before the shot, let your forehead relax and your eyelids settle naturally. A slight lift in the lower lids, like a small genuine smile reaching the eyes, often reads as calmer and more focused.
- **Consider frames if you wear glasses.** A frame with a fairly straight top line echoes a low, straight brow. This is a styling choice, not a correction.

These tips work only in photos and in how you hold your face. They don't alter anatomy, and they aren't meant to.

### 5. Skip the myths, exercises and exact numbers

The hunter-eyes topic attracts confident claims that don't hold up. Before you commit time or money, filter these out:

- **Eye exercises and facial "training."** [No eye exercise changes orbital depth](https://www.stylementor.fashion/2026/02/eye-area-aesthetics-hunter-eyes-guide.html), and skeletal eye-area traits [don't respond to any routine or device](https://www.looksmeter.com/hunter-eyes).
- **Mewing as an eye fix.** Mewing claims concern the upper jaw, and [the evidence for meaningful adult skeletal change is weak](https://www.looksmeter.com/hunter-eyes).
- **Biological "signals."** Some pages state that deep-set eyes [signal high testosterone exposure during development](https://looksmaxxers.com/pages/hunter-eyes-what-they-are-and-how-to-get-them), but they present this as fact without evidence. Treat it as forum lore.
- **Exact tilt targets.** As noted above, there's no agreed cutoff for what counts as hunter-eye tilt. Number targets add anxiety without adding accuracy.

## What about cosmetic procedures?

**Talk to a qualified professional before considering this.** An oculoplastic surgeon (a surgeon who specializes in the eyelids and orbit) can explain what is realistic for your anatomy.

Two facts help set expectations:

- **Eyelid surgery doesn't change bone.** [Blepharoplasty operates on the soft tissue around the eye](https://drturner.com.au/blogs/blepharoplasty-eye-shape/), not on the eyeball, orbit or bony anatomy. Removing excess upper-lid skin can make an eye look *more open* by restoring proportions the skin was hiding. That's often the opposite of what hunter-eye searchers want.
- **Non-surgical options are limited and temporary.** [Makeup, lash extensions, under-eye filler, brow-lifting injectables and certain eye drops can make eyes look more open](https://drkopelman.com/surgery-to-make-eyes-bigger/), but none of these change eyelid anatomy the way surgery does. Most of them push toward a wider look, not a hooded one.

There's also a risk of overshooting. [Exaggerated tilt can look unnatural or "surgical"](https://www.stylementor.fashion/2026/02/eye-area-aesthetics-hunter-eyes-guide.html). One review of the topic notes that [the research backs an upward tilt and a low brow, but not extra lift](https://looksmaxxing.com/hunter-eyes/). More is not automatically better.

## Keep the goal in proportion

The hunter-eyes framework is narrow, and it has blind spots worth knowing about.

- **It handles human variation poorly.** [Every trait in the hunter/prey framework varies substantially between ancestral populations](https://softmaxx.io/blog/hunter-eyes-vs-prey-eyes), including upper-eyelid shape, orbital shape and canthal tilt. A natural difference in lid structure isn't a flaw to correct.
- **It isn't the only attractive eye area.** [The look is not required for attractiveness, and softer, rounder or more open eye areas can also be attractive when they fit the rest of the face](https://lookmax-analyzer.com/looksmaxxingpedia/hunter-eyes).
- **It's male-coded.** Forums mostly apply the term to men. Many people, including many women, prefer a more open, lifted eye area, and that preference is as legitimate as any forum ideal.

A useful reframe is to stop asking "how do I get hunter eyes?" and ask "how do I present the eye area I have at its best?" The second question has practical answers: honest photos, deliberate brow grooming, good lighting and a relaxed expression.

## Quick checklist

1. Learn the four traits behind the label and check which ones you already have.
2. Assess your eyes with straight-on, eye-level, relaxed photos, and treat inconsistent results as uncertain.
3. Groom brows to keep them full, low and fairly straight, unless you prefer a different shape.
4. Light photos from slightly above, keep the camera at eye level, and avoid widening your eyes.
5. Ignore exercises, mewing promises and exact degree targets.
6. Discuss any procedure with a qualified oculoplastic surgeon, and expect soft-tissue changes, not new bone structure.
