A double chin is one of the most common things people dislike about their profile. According to the American Society for Dermatologic Surgery, as cited by [Johns Hopkins Medicine](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/health/wellness-and-prevention/double-chin-surgery-what-you-need-to-know), as many as 68 percent of people are bothered by theirs. So if you're searching for how to get rid of a double chin, you're in large company.

The frustrating part is that most advice treats it as one problem with one fix: do these five exercises, use this roller, eat less. In reality, the fullness under your chin can come from several different causes. The fix that works for one person does little for another. This guide sorts out the causes, separates what has evidence from what doesn't, and gives you a practical plan built around *your* version of the problem.

## What a Double Chin Actually Is

Clinicians call it **submental fullness**. *Submental* simply means "below the chin." The most common component is **submental fat**, a pad of fat that sits between the skin and the muscles under your jaw.

Fat is only one layer, though. What you see in a mirror or photo is the combined result of several things:

- **The fat pad** under the chin
- **The skin** covering it, and how firm or loose it is
- **The bony structure** of your jaw and chin, which sets how much room that tissue has
- **Your head and neck position** at the moment you're seen or photographed

That's why two people with the same body weight can have very different profiles. It's also why a single universal fix doesn't exist.

## The Main Causes, and Why They Matter

Johns Hopkins Medicine lists the causes as hereditary factors, weight, anatomy and airway positioning. A peer-reviewed anatomy paper in the [PMC archive](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12163971/) groups double chin deformity into three broad categories:

1. Congenital factors, meaning traits you're born with
2. Iatrogenic causes, meaning changes from previous procedures such as jaw or chin surgery
3. Excess submental fat accumulation

Here is what each looks like in everyday terms.

### Body fat

When you gain weight, fat increases across the body, including under the chin. If your chin fullness grew along with your weight, this is likely a major factor for you.

### Inherited fat distribution

Genetics decide where your body stores fat. A patient education resource from Medanta notes that in many people, family predisposition causes submental fat to sit under the chin *independent of body weight*. If a parent has a similar profile at a healthy weight, this may be you.

### Jaw and chin structure

A chin that sits further back or a shorter jawline gives soft tissue less support. The same amount of fat then looks fuller. This is anatomy, not something you did.

### Loose skin

Skin loses firmness over time, and it can stay stretched after significant weight loss. Medanta points out that losing fat does not address skin laxity, so loose skin is a separate issue from fat.

### Head and neck position

How you hold your head changes how much the tissue under your chin bunches up. Phone use, laptop screens and low camera angles all push the chin down toward the neck.

### A quick self-check (editorial suggestion, not a diagnosis)

- **Compare old photos.** Did your chin fullness change with your weight, or has it always been there?
- **Look at family photos.** A similar profile in lean relatives points toward inheritance or structure.
- **Take a side photo with your head level**, eyes looking straight ahead, phone at eye height. Then take another with your chin dropped. A big difference between the two means posture and angle are a large part of what you see.

## How to Get Rid of a Double Chin: A Practical Plan

These steps start with the lowest-risk changes that affect the most people. They are editorial suggestions built around the causes above, not a guaranteed result.

1. **Identify your main driver.** Use the self-check above. Most people have a mix, but one cause usually dominates.
2. **If body fat is a factor, work on overall fat loss.** You can't choose where fat comes off. Medanta's patient education notes that a sustained calorie deficit reduces submental fat along with fat elsewhere. Pick a way of eating you can keep up for months, not a crash plan.
3. **Set realistic timelines.** Medanta states plainly that submental fat cannot be meaningfully reduced within five days. Treat any "get rid of it in a week" promise as a red flag.
4. **Fix your screen and phone height.** Raise your laptop so the top of the screen is roughly at eye level, and hold your phone higher. This changes how you carry your head for hours every day.
5. **Adjust grooming and styling.** Beard shape, hairstyle, collars and necklines change how the area under your jaw reads. See the styling section below.
6. **Track with consistent photos.** Use the same light, distance and head position every two to four weeks. Daily mirror checks are too noisy to judge progress.

### What "posture" means here

Good posture for your profile isn't about forcing your chin up. Think of your ears roughly stacked over your shoulders, with your head neither jutting forward nor tucked down. A simple chin tuck, gently drawing your head straight back while keeping your eyes level, is a useful reminder of this position. Treat it as a posture habit. There is no good evidence that it removes fat.

## Do Double Chin Exercises Work?

This is where most online advice goes wrong. The short answer: **there is no good evidence that exercises remove fat from under the chin.**

A case report indexed on [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39735145/) tested a commercial jaw exerciser and reviewed the wider literature. The authors found limited evidence that these devices reduce double chins, sharpen jawlines or tighten skin. They also noted that the chewing muscles these devices work do not directly affect submental fat or skin elasticity. The report itself was small, two people with subjective measures, so it isn't proof either way. It does show that the bold marketing claims lack support.

Applied to common advice:

- **Jaw exercisers and chewing gum** work your chewing muscles along the side of the jaw, not the fat pad under your chin.
- **Face yoga and "jawline" routines** might build some muscle tone, but there is no good evidence that they remove submental fat.
- **Rollers and massage tools** have no good evidence for reducing chin fat.

If you enjoy facial exercises, they're low risk. Just don't expect them to replace overall fat loss or change your bone structure.

## Styling and Grooming That Change How It Reads

These are editorial suggestions. They don't change your anatomy, but they change what people see, and they work from day one.

### If you grow facial hair

- A beard can add visual structure along the jaw. Keep the neckline clean and slightly above where your neck meets your jaw, rather than letting it drift down your throat.
- Very wide, rounded beard shapes add bulk to the lower face. Keeping the sides tighter than the chin tends to look more defined.

### Hair and framing

- For longer hair, styles with some movement or length below the jaw break up the horizontal line under the chin.
- Volume at the crown, rather than at the sides, lengthens the overall silhouette of the face.
- Respect your own style preferences. There's no single correct look for any face.

### Clothing

- Open collars and V-necklines draw the eye downward and create a vertical line.
- Tight crew necks and high turtlenecks push tissue upward and can emphasize fullness.

### Photos

- Keep the camera at or slightly above eye level.
- Lengthen your neck slightly and bring your face a little forward, rather than pulling your chin back toward your throat.

## What About Injections, Fat Freezing and Surgery?

You'll see these marketed heavily for double chins, so here is a plain account. **We don't recommend injections, fat-freezing devices, liposuction or face-lifts for appearance.** Put the non-medical options above first.

- **Deoxycholic acid injections (sold as Kybella, plus a generic).** These are prescription drugs that destroy fat cells. The [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-approves-additional-information-labeling-kybella-deoxycholic-acid-injection-warning-adverse) notes that nodules and masses can occur even when injected in the approved area under the chin. It has also recorded serious adverse events, including neuromuscular problems, when the drug was used outside that area.
- **Liposuction and face-lifts.** These are surgery, with incisions, anesthesia, recovery time and the usual surgical risks. Johns Hopkins notes that face-lifts are most often done under general anesthesia.
- **Fat-freezing and other device treatments.** We could not find independent evidence strong enough to report on these here.
- **Topical fat-dissolving creams.** These are experimental. No cream sold for this purpose has the evidence to back its claims.

*Talk to a qualified professional before considering any of these,* and be wary of anyone selling them as a quick fix.

## When to See a Doctor Instead

Most double chins are a cosmetic concern, not a medical one. Some changes under the chin aren't fat at all. See a doctor rather than treating it as an appearance issue if you notice:

- Fullness that appears suddenly or grows quickly
- A firm lump, or swelling only on one side
- Pain, tenderness or difficulty swallowing

## Key Takeaways

- A double chin has several possible causes: body fat, inherited fat distribution, jaw structure, loose skin and head position.
- If fat is the driver, overall fat loss over months is the realistic route. You can't target the chin alone.
- There's no good evidence that exercises, gum or jaw devices remove submental fat.
- Posture, screen height, grooming, clothing and camera angle change how your profile looks right away.
- We don't recommend injections or surgery for appearance. Weigh the risks with a qualified professional if you're considering them.
