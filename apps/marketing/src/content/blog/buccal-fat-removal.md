Buccal fat removal promises a more sculpted, angular midface with a short recovery. For some people, for a while, that holds. For many, the result turns into the opposite of what they wanted, and it is permanent.

This article covers what the procedure does, what goes wrong and why, who tends to regret it, what to try instead, and what options exist if it has already gone wrong.

## What Buccal Fat Removal Actually Does

The buccal fat pad is a distinct pocket of fat deep in the cheek, behind the cheekbone and in front of the jaw muscle. Unlike the fat under the skin, it changes relatively little with diet. It gives the midface volume and supports the soft tissue over it.

In the procedure, a surgeon makes a small cut inside the mouth, the fat pad is pushed through the opening, and part of it is removed. There is no external scar, and it is usually done as a same-day procedure.

Removing it changes the long-term support structure of your midface. That is the part the marketing leaves out.

## What Goes Wrong

### Hollowing, often years later

This is the most common regret. Faces lose volume naturally with age. The buccal fat pad partly buffers that loss. Without it, a face that looked sculpted at 25 can look gaunt, hollow or "skeletal" at 35 or 45. Remove it young, and you borrow against volume you will need later.

### Uneven results

The fat pad is not a uniform structure, and its extensions vary from person to person. Removing different amounts on each side leaves visible asymmetry, and correcting it means more procedures.

### Nerve and duct injury

Branches of the facial nerve, which moves the muscles of the face, run close to the fat pad, and so does the parotid duct, which carries saliva into the mouth. Temporary numbness is relatively common. Permanent weakness of facial movement is rare but has been reported, as have injuries to the duct.

### The wrong face for it

People with already lean, angular faces gain almost nothing and risk the hollow outcome. People whose fullness comes from large jaw muscles, a swollen parotid gland or overall body fat see no improvement at all, because the fat pad is not the cause.

### Less change than expected

The visible change is usually subtle. People expecting a different face and getting a slightly slimmer one are disappointed even without a complication.

## What People Who Regret It Have in Common

1. **They were young at the time of surgery.** Younger faces often still slim naturally, and they have decades of age-related volume loss ahead.
2. **They followed a trend.** Fashions in faces change. A removed fat pad does not come back.
3. **They did not fully grasp that it was permanent.** Fat grafting can partly replace volume, but it is another procedure with its own unpredictability.
4. **They chose on price or speed.** Quick consultations and package deals are a common thread.

## Start Without Surgery

Facial fullness usually has causes that need no surgery at all:

- **Age.** Faces commonly slim through the twenties as the "baby face" fullness of adolescence fades. What looks round at 20 often looks different at 28.
- **Body fat.** Overall body fat shows in the face. Lowering it slims the cheeks and sharpens the jaw.
- **Water retention.** Salt, alcohol and poor sleep make the face puffy, especially in the morning. Cutting back shows within days.
- **Hairstyle and beard.** Both change the perceived shape and width of the face. A beard that adds structure along the jaw makes the midface look less full.
- **Photos.** Phone cameras held close make faces look rounder and wider than they are. Judge from photos taken at a normal distance.

Some "non-surgical" options people suggest are medical procedures too, such as Botox injections into the jaw muscles. They have their own side effects, and we don't recommend them for appearance either.

## If You Are Still Considering It

If you have weighed all of this, these warning signs in a clinic should stop you:

- **No proper in-person consultation**, or a very short one.
- **Before and after portfolios with only young patients**, photographed shortly after surgery. Ask to see patients your age, years later.
- **Guarantees about results.** No ethical surgeon guarantees a cosmetic outcome.
- **Package deals** that bundle it with other procedures without discussing your own anatomy.
- **Pressure to book quickly** or discounts for same-day commitment.

Ask any surgeon to show you how your face is likely to age with and without the fat pad, and give yourself at least three months between your first serious research and any booking.

## If It Has Already Gone Wrong

If you have already had buccal fat removed and are unhappy with the result, you have options, though none are simple. They are medical procedures to discuss with a board-certified plastic surgeon, ideally one experienced in revisions:

- **Fat grafting:** fat is taken from elsewhere in your body and injected into the hollow area. Part of it is always reabsorbed, so several sessions may be needed.
- **Fillers:** can temporarily restore volume, but need repeating and carry their own risks near the blood vessels of the face.
- **Cheek implants:** a more involved option for significant hollowing, with the risks of any implant.
- **Waiting:** swelling and settling in the first six to twelve months change how the result looks. Give it time before deciding on revision, unless far too much was removed.

## The Bigger Picture

Buccal fat removal gone wrong is not a rare anomaly. It is the predictable result of an irreversible procedure becoming fashionable faster than the evidence supporting it. The surgery itself is simple. Living with a missing fat pad for the rest of your life is not.
