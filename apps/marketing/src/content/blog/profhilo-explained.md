Profhilo comes up quickly once you start reading about skin quality. You see it on clinic menus, injector social accounts and forum threads asking whether it is "filler for people who don't want filler." The name gets used loosely, and much of what is written about it comes from clinics that sell it.

This guide covers what the product is, what the published data covers, where the gaps are, and what you can work on without a needle.

**Our position up front: we don't recommend Profhilo for appearance.** The rest of this article explains why, and what we would focus on first.

## The Short Answer

- **What it is:** an injectable hyaluronic acid product made by the Italian company IBSA and marketed for skin quality, crepiness and laxity. It is not a classic volumising filler.
- **Our stance:** we don't recommend it for appearance. The main risks are listed below.
- **What we'd put first:** sleep, daily sun protection, a simple and consistent skincare routine, body fat level, grooming, hairstyle and posture. These are free or cheap, carry no injection risk, and you can reverse any of them.

The main risks are:

- Injection-site bumps, swelling, redness, tenderness and bruising.
- Rare but serious vascular events, which are a risk with any hyaluronic acid injectable.
- An evidence base that is small and largely linked to the manufacturer.
- No FDA approval in the United States.

## What Profhilo Actually Is

**Hyaluronic acid (HA)** is a naturally occurring molecule in skin. Chemically it is a glycosaminoglycan, a long chain of sugar units, and it holds a large amount of water. That is why it shows up in everything from serums to injectables.

Profhilo combines two sizes of HA chain:

- **High-molecular-weight HA:** long chains, associated with hydration and a cushioning, gel-like feel.
- **Low-molecular-weight HA:** shorter chains that the manufacturer positions as signalling to skin cells.

In a published neck study, the product used was a 2 ml prefilled syringe of 3.2% HA, with 32 mg of each type in saline ([Sparavigna et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9509274/)). IBSA calls the result "hybrid cooperative complexes."

### Why it is not usually called a filler

Most HA fillers are **cross-linked**. Their HA chains are chemically bonded into a firmer gel that holds its shape in one spot and adds volume. Profhilo is not cross-linked in that way.

Clinic sources such as Centre for Surgery and Dr Christine describe it as spreading through the skin rather than volumising or contouring. That is a description of intent, not proof of a visible outcome.

### The product family

"Profhilo" now covers more than one product:

- **Profhilo (face):** the original 2 ml product described above.
- **Profhilo Body:** made with the same technology, containing 48 mg of HA, according to a manufacturer-linked safety update.
- **Profhilo Structura:** positioned differently. Its clinical trial registration describes a specific indication for restoring fat compartments and counteracting facial volume loss.

If a forum post says "Profhilo did X," it is worth asking which one they mean.

### How clinics typically deliver it

Clinic guides describe a fairly standard routine:

1. **Two sessions, four weeks apart**, as the starting course.
2. **The Bio Aesthetic Points (BAP) technique:** five small injection points on each side of the face.
3. **Changes described over weeks, not days.** Small bumps at the injection points are expected on the day.

These details come from clinics, not from regulators. Treat them as a description of common practice, not a standard.

## What the Evidence Does and Doesn't Show

![3D cross-section block of skin in soft pinks and creams, showing a thin outer epidermis, a thick dermis with wavy collagen-like fibres and purple fibroblast-like cells with faint translucent blue gel spread through it, and a layer of yellow fat cells at the bottom.](/blog/profhilo-explained/2.webp)

The published research is real, but it is narrow. Here is what exists:

- **A neck study of 25 people aged 40 to 65.** Each had two injections 30 days apart, with check-ups at one and four months. The author list includes people affiliated with IBSA.
- **An open-label study in Asian patients.** Its own authors describe the clinical data on Profhilo in Asian facial skin as limited.
- **Pilot studies on Profhilo Structura.** One observational pilot on the lateral cheek reported no serious adverse events.
- **A registered trial of Profhilo Structura** on ClinicalTrials.gov. Its main outcome is the share of participants with adverse device effects, and it compares results with a control group.

One injector education site summed the evidence up as encouraging. It also said the evidence does not yet match the controlled, multicenter data behind the US approval of a different skin-quality product. We think that is a fair reading.

### How to read these studies

Three terms help:

- **Open-label:** everyone knows who got the treatment. This makes subjective ratings, like "skin looks better," easier to sway.
- **Manufacturer-linked:** the company that sells the product helped run or analyse the study. This doesn't make the findings false. It is a reason to want independent replication.
- **Durability:** clinics often quote how long results last. That injector education site notes there is no FDA-reviewed durability claim for Profhilo. Any "lasts X months" figure you see is a clinic estimate.

## Side Effects and Risks

The most common side effects are tied to the injection itself:

- Small raised bumps at the injection points
- Swelling
- Redness
- Tenderness
- Bruising

A manufacturer-linked safety update states that for HA injectables in general, the most common adverse events come from the injection procedure and are usually temporary ([safety update on hybrid cooperative complexes](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12257262/)).

### Where the safety numbers come from

The large safety figures for Profhilo come from **post-marketing surveillance**. That means reports that doctors and clinics choose to send in after treating patients, not from people tracked in a trial.

- An earlier analysis reviewed spontaneous reports sent in after the product's 2015 launch. The authors acknowledged that underreporting of minor events cannot be ruled out ([Cassuto et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7327616/)).
- A summary of the later update on an injector education site estimated about 1.09 million patients were exposed. It also noted one report each of vascular occlusion and embolism.
- That same summary points out that this kind of voluntary reporting always undercounts events.

**Vascular occlusion** means injected material blocking or compressing a blood vessel. It is rare, but it is the serious risk shared by HA injectables.

### Warning signs that need urgent care

One clinic safety guide lists the signs that need urgent medical assessment after any injectable:

- Severe pain that keeps increasing
- Blanching, meaning skin turning pale or white
- Mottled or dusky skin
- Any other change that develops quickly

### Health history matters

The FDA's general guidance on dermal fillers, as summarised by First Derm, asks people to tell the provider about several conditions:

- An active skin infection or inflammation
- A bleeding disorder
- Severe allergies or a history of anaphylaxis

## Regulatory Status: Where You Live Matters

Profhilo is widely used outside the United States. Inside the US, it is **unapproved, not off-label.**

- **Off-label:** a product approved for one use, used for another.
- **Unapproved:** a product that has no FDA approval for any use. Profhilo falls into this group.

IBSA has licensed US development rights to Evolus, which has taken on the clinical work needed to seek approval. No approval date has been announced.

Two things are worth keeping straight:

- **No approval does not mean proven harmful.** It means the FDA has not reviewed the product for safety or effectiveness.
- **In the US, don't get it.** A product with no FDA approval for any use is not something to have injected for appearance, and a US clinic offering it raises questions about where the product came from and how it was stored.

US readers may also see Skinvive mentioned as a comparison. It is a separate FDA-approved product that uses cross-linked HA microdroplets, so it does not work in the same way.

## What Changes How Your Skin Looks, No Needle Required

These are editorial suggestions, not measured outcomes. They are where we would spend effort first. Each is low-risk and easy to reverse.

### Skin basics

- **Sun protection every day.** Broad-spectrum SPF, shade and a hat. UV exposure is one of the factors researchers link to visible skin ageing.
- **A boring, consistent routine.** Gentle cleanser, moisturiser, sunscreen. Consistency over months beats rotating through new products.
- **Sleep and hydration.** Poor sleep shows up quickly around the eyes and in how dull skin looks in photos.
- **Not smoking.** Smoking is widely associated with worse skin quality.

### Face framing

- **Body fat level.** Facial definition changes with overall body composition far more than with any skin treatment.
- **Hairstyle.** A cut that suits your face shape and hair texture changes how your whole face reads.
- **Facial hair and brows.** Neat grooming sharpens edges. A well-shaped beard can define the jawline area visually.
- **Glasses frames.** Frame size and shape interact with face proportions.
- **Posture and lighting.** Head and neck position, and the direction of light, change how skin texture and jawline show up in photos.

## If a Clinic Brings It Up Anyway

**Decline.** We don't recommend Profhilo for appearance, and a clinic suggesting it doesn't change the limited evidence or the risks described above. In the United States, it is unapproved, so don't get it there at all.

You don't need to prepare questions, compare offers or plan a course. Saying no is enough. Put that time and money into the non-needle basics above instead.

If you have a genuine skin condition or medical concern, that is a separate matter for a doctor, not a reason to book a cosmetic injectable. And if you have already had any injectable and notice the warning signs listed earlier, seek urgent medical care.

## The Bottom Line

Profhilo is a non-cross-linked hyaluronic acid injectable with a real but limited evidence base. Most of the studies are small, open-label or manufacturer-linked. Its safety record comes mainly from voluntary reports, which undercount problems.

It is not approved in the US. Like any HA injectable, it carries a rare but serious risk of vascular events.

We don't recommend it for appearance. Sun protection, sleep, a steady skincare routine, body composition and smart grooming are where the low-risk changes are. Start there, and judge them over months, not days.
