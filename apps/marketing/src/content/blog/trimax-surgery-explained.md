If you've spent any time around looksmaxxing discussions, you've seen **trimax surgery** described as the heavy hitter of facial procedures. People bring it up in conversations about forward growth, a stronger jawline, and a more balanced profile. It is also a major operation with a long timeline, real risks, and a recovery that asks a lot of you.

This guide explains what trimax surgery involves, who it is designed for, how the process unfolds from first consult to final result, and which questions are worth asking before you commit to anything.

> **Talk to a qualified professional before considering this.** This article is educational and is not medical advice. Only a board-certified oral and maxillofacial surgeon who has examined you, reviewed your imaging, and discussed your goals can tell you whether any jaw procedure is appropriate for you.

## What Trimax Surgery Actually Is

"Trimax" is an informal term. You won't find it in surgical textbooks. Surgeons usually call the same operation **bimaxillary orthognathic surgery with genioplasty**, or simply double jaw surgery with a chin osteotomy. *Orthognathic* comes from Greek roots meaning "straight jaws," and an *osteotomy* is a controlled surgical cut in bone.

Trimax combines three osteotomies in a single operation:

1. **Le Fort I osteotomy.** The upper jaw (maxilla) is cut horizontally above the tooth roots, freed, and repositioned forward, backward, up, down, or rotated.
2. **Bilateral sagittal split osteotomy (BSSO).** The lower jaw (mandible) is split on both sides behind the molars so the tooth-bearing part can be advanced, set back, or rotated.
3. **Osseous genioplasty.** The chin bone is cut and moved independently, usually forward, but sometimes down, up, or sideways to correct asymmetry.

The repositioned segments are held in place with small titanium plates and screws while the bone heals.

### How trimax differs from bimax

Bimax refers to the first two cuts only: upper and lower jaw. The chin gets added because moving the jaws to fix the bite doesn't always put the chin where it looks or functions best. Moving the chin separately lets a surgeon fine-tune projection and vertical height without compromising how the teeth meet.

### Where the term gets confusing

Online, some people use "trimax" loosely to mean bimax plus other work, like cheekbone (zygoma) procedures or implants. If you raise the term with a surgeon, describe exactly what you mean. Precise language leads to a clearer plan.

## Who Trimax Is Designed For

Orthognathic surgery was developed to solve **functional problems**, and that is still how most surgeons approach it. Common indications include:

- **Skeletal malocclusion.** A bite problem caused by jaw position rather than tooth position alone. *Class II* means the lower jaw sits too far back relative to the upper. *Class III* means the lower jaw sits too far forward or the upper jaw is underdeveloped.
- **Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA).** Advancing both jaws, a procedure called maxillomandibular advancement (MMA), can enlarge the airway behind the tongue and soft palate. Research suggests MMA is one of the more effective surgical options for moderate to severe OSA in selected patients.
- **Facial asymmetry.** When one side of the jaw grew differently from the other.
- **Open bite or long lower face.** Often linked to excess vertical growth of the maxilla, which can also cause a gummy smile.
- **Retruded midface or chin.** A flat middle face or weak chin that also affects function.

### The aesthetic angle

Many people interested in trimax are motivated by appearance: more forward projection, a sharper jaw angle, better chin definition, and improved lip support. A concept that comes up often is **counterclockwise rotation**. Viewed from the right side, the whole upper and lower jaw complex is rotated so the chin moves up and forward. This can shorten a long lower face and increase chin projection.

### Functional vs. aesthetic candidates

The distinction matters in practice. If you have a documented bite problem or sleep apnea, insurance may cover part of the treatment and surgeons will usually see a clear medical rationale. If your bite is normal and your goals are purely cosmetic, fewer surgeons will perform full trimax, costs are typically out of pocket, and the risk-to-benefit calculation deserves extra scrutiny.

## How the Process Works, Phase by Phase

Trimax is not a single appointment. It is a treatment journey that often spans one to three years.

### Phase 1: Consultation and records

Expect detailed imaging and measurements: a **cone beam CT (CBCT) scan** for 3D bone anatomy, standardized facial photos, dental impressions or intraoral scans, and **cephalometric analysis**. That last one measures angles and distances between skeletal landmarks to quantify where your jaws sit relative to the skull base.

### Phase 2: Pre-surgical orthodontics

Most patients wear braces or aligners for roughly 12 to 18 months before surgery. The goal is *decompensation*: moving teeth back to their natural positions over the bone. Your bite may look or feel worse during this phase. That is expected, because the surgery will correct the skeletal problem underneath. Some practices offer a **surgery-first approach** that shortens this phase, but it isn't right for everyone.

### Phase 3: Virtual surgical planning

Many surgeons now use **virtual surgical planning (VSP)**. Your CT data is loaded into 3D software, bone movements are simulated to the millimeter, and custom splints or cutting guides are fabricated. VSP helps with precision and gives you a preview of the planned skeletal changes. Soft tissue predictions are approximations, not guarantees.

### Phase 4: Surgery day

The operation is done under general anesthesia, typically through incisions inside the mouth, so there are usually no visible facial scars. Combined three-segment surgery commonly takes several hours. Most patients stay in the hospital for one to three nights.

### Phase 5: Post-surgical orthodontics

After healing, braces stay on for several more months, commonly six to twelve, to settle the final bite.

## Recovery Timeline

Every patient heals differently, but a typical trajectory looks like this:

- **Days 1 to 3:** Swelling peaks. Ice, head elevation, and prescribed medications are the focus. Nasal congestion is common after Le Fort I surgery.
- **Weeks 1 to 2:** Liquid or blended diet. Bruising may spread down the neck. Many people feel exhausted and lose weight.
- **Weeks 2 to 6:** Gradual shift to a soft, no-chew diet. Many desk workers return to work in two to four weeks.
- **Weeks 6 to 12:** Initial bone healing allows a slow return to chewing, guided by your surgeon.
- **Months 3 to 6:** Most visible swelling resolves.
- **Around 12 months:** Residual swelling fades and the final result becomes clear.

**Numbness** in the lower lip, chin, and sometimes the upper lip and cheeks is very common early on. Sensation often returns gradually over months, though for some people it does not return fully.

## Risks and Tradeoffs

Trimax has a strong track record in experienced hands, but it is major surgery. Honest risk awareness is part of making a good decision.

- **Nerve injury.** The *inferior alveolar nerve* runs through the lower jaw and supplies feeling to the lower lip and chin. BSSO places it at risk, and genioplasty adds risk to its branch, the *mental nerve*. Research suggests altered sensation is common right after surgery and that most patients improve, but a minority report lasting changes.
- **Relapse.** Bones can drift partway back toward their original position, especially with large movements.
- **Jaw joint (TMJ) symptoms.** Some patients see improvement, others develop new clicking, pain, or stiffness.
- **Nasal changes.** Moving the maxilla can widen the base of the nose or tilt the tip upward. Surgeons often use an *alar cinch* suture to limit widening.
- **Hardware, infection, and bleeding.** Plates occasionally need removal. Infection and significant bleeding are uncommon but possible.
- **Psychological adjustment.** Seeing a noticeably different face in the mirror can be harder than people expect, even when the result is objectively good. If you notice yourself fixating heavily on small flaws, it is worth discussing that with a mental health professional before surgery.

## Cost and Insurance

Costs vary widely by country, city, surgeon, and whether orthodontics, hospital fees, anesthesia, and imaging are bundled. In the United States, total out-of-pocket figures commonly reported for double jaw surgery plus orthodontics run from the tens of thousands of dollars upward.

Insurance may cover orthognathic surgery when there is documented medical necessity, such as a significant malocclusion or sleep apnea. Genioplasty is more often classified as cosmetic and excluded, even when the jaw surgery itself is covered. Get pre-authorization in writing. If you're considering surgery abroad, factor in travel for follow-ups and what happens if a complication arises after you return home.

## Alternatives Worth Knowing About

Trimax is the most comprehensive option, not the only one. Depending on your anatomy, something smaller may address most of your goal.

| Option | What it changes | Invasiveness |
|---|---|---|
| Orthodontic camouflage | Tooth position, some lip support | Low |
| Sliding genioplasty alone | Chin projection and height | Moderate |
| Custom jaw or chin implants | Jawline width and contour | Moderate |
| Bimax without genioplasty | Bite, midface, and jaw position | High |
| Dermal fillers | Temporary contour at jaw and chin | Low |

Non-surgical factors matter too. Lower body fat, neck posture, facial hair styling, and good sleep may all change how defined your jawline appears. Claims that tongue posture techniques can reshape adult bone have limited scientific support, so treat them as a habit to explore, not a surgical substitute.

## Practical Tips Before You Book a Consultation

If you're seriously exploring trimax surgery, these steps can help you walk into consultations prepared and walk out with a clearer answer.

1. **Document your baseline.** Take front, three-quarter, and profile photos in consistent lighting with a neutral expression. An app like [Aura](https://app.aura-looksmaxxing.com) can give you an objective read on jawline definition and facial proportions, which may help you describe your goals more precisely and track changes over time. It is not a diagnostic tool. A surgeon's imaging and cephalometric analysis determine candidacy.
2. **Choose the right specialist.** Look for a board-certified oral and maxillofacial surgeon who performs orthognathic surgery regularly. Ask how many double jaw cases they do each year.
3. **Ask to see similar cases.** Request before and after photos of patients with anatomy and goals close to yours, not just their best results.
4. **Rule out sleep apnea.** If you snore, wake up tired, or have been told you stop breathing at night, get a sleep study. It could change both the surgical plan and insurance coverage.
5. **Get at least two opinions.** Different surgeons may recommend different movements, or a smaller procedure entirely.
6. **Ask specific questions.** How much will each segment move? How do you manage the nasal base? What are your nerve injury and relapse rates? What is the plan if hardware needs removal?
7. **Plan the logistics.** Arrange two to four weeks off work, a support person for the first week, a good blender, and a financial buffer for extra appointments.
8. **Check your motivation.** The best outcomes tend to come from patients with realistic expectations and clear functional or aesthetic goals, not from chasing a specific score or an idealized face.

## The Bottom Line

Trimax surgery repositions the upper jaw, lower jaw, and chin in one operation. For people with genuine skeletal imbalance, bite problems, or sleep apnea, it may meaningfully improve both function and facial harmony. It also comes with a long orthodontic timeline, a demanding recovery, and risks like nerve changes and relapse that deserve serious weight. Gather objective information, consult experienced specialists, compare your options, and make the decision on your own timeline.
