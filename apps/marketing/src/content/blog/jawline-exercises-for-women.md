## The Short Answer Before the Routines

You probably searched for jawline exercises because you want a cleaner, more defined lower face. Plenty of routines promise exactly that, with daily moves, chewing gadgets and before-and-after timelines.

The evidence is thinner than the marketing. Jaw and face exercises work the muscles. The outline you see in the mirror comes mostly from **bone, skin and the soft tissue over it**, and studies have not shown that exercise reshapes that outline in any reliable way. Some of the most popular methods can also strain your jaw joint if you overdo them.

That doesn't make the whole topic a dead end. This guide covers:

- How the jawline is built, and why muscle work has limits
- What the studies on jaw and face exercises actually found
- Which methods to skip
- A gentle routine, if you still want one, with clear stop rules
- Styling choices that change how your jawline reads, starting today

## How Your Jawline Is Built

To judge any exercise, it helps to know what it is working on.

### The bone

The **mandible** is your lower jawbone. Its angle, width and chin projection set the basic frame of your lower face. In adults, exercise does not change bone shape. Cleveland Clinic makes the same point about mewing, the tongue-posture trend: it isn't specific or strong enough to significantly change dental or facial features.

### The muscles

Four main muscles move your jaw when you chew:

- **Masseter**: the thick muscle at the back corner of your jaw. You can feel it tighten when you clench.
- **Temporalis**: a fan-shaped muscle at your temples.
- **Medial and lateral pterygoids**: deeper muscles inside the jaw that help with grinding and side-to-side movement.

Most "jawline workouts" target these chewing muscles. The cheek muscles are separate, and they are what face yoga routines usually work.

### The soft tissue

Skin, fat and connective tissue sit over all of this. The **submental area**, the space under your chin, is where a "double chin" shows up. How much fat sits in your lower face, and where, is strongly shaped by genetics. One dental practice that reviewed jaw exercisers put it plainly: your body loses fat according to genetic patterns, so jaw exercises won't slim the cheeks.

### Why this matters

If training adds anything, it adds **muscle volume**. In the best-known facial exercise study (covered below), the results suggested the improvements [may come from exercise-driven growth of the cheek muscles](https://www.mdedge.com/edermatologynews/article/155854/aesthetic-dermatology/facial-exercises-improved-appearance-small). That change was *fuller* cheeks. Many people searching for jawline exercises want the opposite: less fullness and a crisper edge.

## What the Research Actually Shows

The evidence on jawline exercises for women is small and mixed. The studies below are the ones worth knowing.

### Jaw workout products showed no jawline change

A [case report examining jaw exercise products](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11681191/) tracked people using these devices for facial contouring. The authors recorded **no change in jawline enhancement**. They stated that the chewing muscles had no direct effect on double-chin fat in their cases. They also concluded there is no strong scientific data showing that jaw workouts reduce submental fat.

It is a very small report, and the authors note that its results are subjective. They call for larger, more objective studies. So treat it as a warning sign about product claims, not a final verdict.

### The face yoga study everyone cites

The study behind most "face yoga works" headlines was a 2018 *JAMA Dermatology* study. Its details are reported across several summaries ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/01/180103160142.htm), [RealSelf](https://www.realself.com/news/face-yoga-for-anti-aging), [AAU](https://www.aau.edu/research-scholarship/featured-research-topics/facial-exercises-help-middle-aged-women-appear-more)):

- **Who:** 27 middle-aged women aged 40 to 65 were recruited, and 16 did all the exercises for the entire study.
- **What:** 32 facial exercises, about a minute each.
- **How often:** 30 minutes daily for the first 8 weeks, then 30 minutes every other day for the final 12 weeks.
- **Result:** The women's average estimated age started at 50.8 years, dropped to 49.6 at eight weeks and to 48.1 at 20 weeks. Raters found that **upper and lower cheek fullness** was significantly enhanced.

This study did not measure jawline definition. It also had no comparison group: [everyone did the exercises and nobody served as a control](https://acibademinternational.com/blog/face-taping-and-face-yoga-do-the-viral-wrinkle-hacks-work/), so it can't show what would have happened without them. The researchers themselves listed the small sample and the all-middle-aged-women group as limitations. It is interesting, but it is not proof that face exercises sculpt the jaw.

Other studies point in different directions. The case report above mentions a controlled study that found no significant difference in wrinkles or sagging between exercise and control groups.

### Where jaw exercises do have a real use

Jaw exercises have a more serious place in treating **temporomandibular disorders (TMD)**. TMD covers pain and dysfunction in the jaw joint and the chewing muscles.

A [systematic review of exercise therapy for pain-related TMD](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10382173/) found that exercise appears effective for TMD muscle and joint pain. It also said this evidence still needs to be firmly established, and that the exercise methods across studies were not standardized. Researchers have also run a [randomized trial of an 8-week neck exercise program](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11773986/) in women with chronic TMD.

The takeaway is that jaw exercises belong to function and pain care. That is a different goal from aesthetics, and it works best with a clinician who chooses the specific exercises.

## What to Skip

Some popular methods carry more risk than reward.

- **Jaw exerciser devices and chewing balls.** Biting against resistance repeatedly puts stress on the temporomandibular joint, the hinge connecting your jaw to your skull. Joana Forsea, DDS, chief of orthodontics at Northwell Health, warns that jaw exercises done in excess can lead to TMJ problems, dental breakdown and periodontal (gum) problems.
- **Mewing to reshape your face.** Cleveland Clinic states that research doesn't support mewing for reshaping and that it can worsen jaw tension and TMJ disorders.
- **Routines built on clenching.** Any move that has you clench hard or hold tension for long stretches works against a relaxed, healthy jaw.
- **Timeline promises.** If a program tells you the week your jaw will look different, that number did not come from research.

## A Gentle Routine, If You Still Want One

Some people like a few minutes of face and neck awareness as part of a skincare or wind-down habit. That's reasonable, as long as you treat it as a **habit check, not a reshaping program**. None of these moves is proven to change your jawline. They are editorial suggestions chosen because they are low-effort and low-load.

![Woman in a grey t-shirt and black leggings sitting upright on a wooden chair in profile beside a bright window, head level and shoulders relaxed, demonstrating a gentle seated chin tuck](/blog/jawline-exercises-for-women/2.webp)

1. **Resting jaw check.** Close your lips with your teeth slightly apart and your tongue resting softly in your mouth. Let your cheeks and temples go loose. The point is to notice clenching you didn't know you were doing. This is not mewing, so don't press your tongue hard against the roof of your mouth.
2. **Slow mirror opening.** Facing a mirror, open your mouth slowly a short, comfortable distance, then close. Watch whether your jaw opens straight. Do a handful of slow repetitions and stay well inside your comfort range.
3. **Seated chin tuck.** Sit tall and gently draw your head straight back, as if making a slight double chin, then relax. Keep it small and slow. This is a neck awareness move, not a jawline sculptor.
4. **Cheek lift, if you enjoy face yoga.** Smile gently with closed lips, feel your cheeks rise, hold briefly and release. This is the muscle area the face yoga study changed, so know that the effect it showed was fullness, not slimming.

### Stop rules

- Stop if you feel pain, clicking, popping, locking or a jaw that gets stuck open or closed.
- Stop if you get headaches or ear pain after the routine.
- If you already have jaw pain, skip online routines. See a dentist or doctor, who can recommend specific exercises that suit your situation.

## What Changes How Your Jawline Reads

Exercise has weak evidence, but styling has an immediate, visible effect on how your lower face comes across. A soft jawline is also not a flaw. Many feminine faces have a gentle lower third, and plenty of women prefer it. These suggestions are for shaping *how* your jaw reads in the direction you want, whatever that is.

### Hair framing

The ends and layers of your hair draw lines across your face. Editorial suggestions:

- **Face-framing layers** that start around the cheekbones lead the eye along the side of the face toward the jaw.
- **Lengths ending at or just below the jaw** emphasize the jaw edge. Lengths ending well above or below it shift attention elsewhere.
- **Some volume at the crown** balances a fuller lower face. Hair that is flat on top and wide at the bottom adds weight lower down.

### Necklines and accessories

- **V-necks and open collars** create a vertical line below the chin. High, tight crew necks can make the chin-to-neck area look shorter.
- **Drop or linear earrings** add vertical lines beside the jaw. Large round studs add width at that level.

### Makeup (optional)

If you wear makeup, a **soft contour shade just under the jawbone**, blended down toward the neck, adds shadow where the jaw meets the neck. A touch of highlight at the center of the chin brings that point forward. Blend well, since a hard stripe reads as makeup rather than shadow.

### Photo angles

In photos, a camera slightly above eye level and a small forward-and-down chin position tend to define the jaw edge. A camera below the chin does the opposite. This is common photography practice rather than anatomy, but it explains why your jaw can look so different from one photo to the next.

## Practical Tips: A Sensible Plan

If you want to act on your jawline goal without wasting effort or risking your jaw:

- **Reset your expectations.** No exercise has solid evidence for slimming the lower face or sharpening the jaw outline.
- **Choose comfort over intensity.** If you do face or jaw moves, keep them short, slow and pain-free. Skip resistance devices.
- **Notice clenching.** Check in a few times a day for a tight jaw, especially when you're at a screen or under stress.
- **Start with styling.** Hair framing, necklines, earrings and optional contour change how your jaw reads right away.
- **Accept your genetics.** Fat distribution in the face is largely genetic, and jaw work does not spot-reduce it.
- **Get pain checked.** Jaw pain, clicking or locking calls for a dentist or doctor, not a harder routine.

## When to Talk to a Professional

See a dentist or doctor if you have ongoing jaw pain, clicking or locking, headaches tied to chewing or clenching, or changes in your bite. For concerns about bite alignment or the structure of your teeth and palate, Cleveland Clinic recommends a dentist or orthodontist rather than at-home techniques. A professional can also tell you whether jaw exercises suit you at all and design them around your situation.

On appearance, jawline exercises for women are mostly about managing expectations. Use gentle habits if you enjoy them, avoid anything that strains your jaw, and lean on styling for visible change.
