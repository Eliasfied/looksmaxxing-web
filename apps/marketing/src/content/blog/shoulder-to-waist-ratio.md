Spend a few minutes in any physique forum and you will see the same number: **1.618**. It is called the golden ratio, the Adonis index or the V-taper target. It is presented as the shoulder-to-waist proportion that makes a body look its best.

The real picture is more useful than the meme. Shoulder to waist ratio is a simple, trackable number. Studies do link a broader-shouldered, narrower-waisted torso with higher attractiveness ratings. But recent data does not point to 1.618. It also shows that one part of the equation does far more work than the other.

This guide covers how to measure your ratio properly, what the studies found, where the popular advice goes wrong, and how to improve the number in ways that also make sense for your training.

## What Shoulder to Waist Ratio Measures

Shoulder to waist ratio (often shortened to **SWR**) [compares the circumference of your shoulders with the circumference of your waist](https://bodyshapecalculator.net/shoulder-to-waist-ratio-calculator/). A higher number means your shoulders are wider relative to your waist. Visually, that reads as a V-shaped torso.

**Shoulder circumference** is the full distance around your upper body at the widest point of your shoulders. The tape passes over the outside of both deltoids, across your upper back and across your chest. It is not the straight-line width from one shoulder to the other.

Researchers often study a close relative, the **shoulder-to-hip ratio (SHR)**. It compares shoulder size with hip size. Both ratios describe the same basic shape: wide on top, narrower lower down.

SHR is [sexually dimorphic](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-031-08956-5_285-1.pdf), meaning it differs on average between men and women. Men with high SHRs have a V-shaped torso, with shoulders wider than the hip region.

### How to Calculate It

The formula is simple:

**Shoulder circumference ÷ waist circumference = shoulder to waist ratio**

If your shoulders measure 120 cm around and your waist measures 80 cm, your ratio is 1.50. Use the same unit for both measurements and the unit does not matter.

To see how demanding the popular targets are, work backwards from your waist. For example, with an 80 cm waist:

- **A ratio of 1.57** requires a shoulder circumference of about 126 cm (80 × 1.57 = 125.6).
- **A ratio of 1.618** requires a shoulder circumference of about 129 cm (80 × 1.618 = 129.4).

For this waist, that roughly 3 cm of shoulder circumference is the difference between the peak in a recent study and the number repeated online. The gap scales with your waist: the larger your waist, the larger the gap in centimetres.

## How to Measure Your Ratio Accurately

Your tape technique matters as much as your physique. A slightly tilted tape or a sucked-in stomach can move your ratio more than a month of training. Follow the same routine every time:

1. **Use a soft tailor's tape**, not a rigid metal tape.
2. **Stand tall and relaxed** with your arms hanging at your sides. Don't flare your lats or puff your chest.
3. **Measure your shoulders** all the way around your upper body at the widest point of your deltoids, the rounded muscles that cap each shoulder. Keep the tape level the whole way around. A second person makes this much easier.
4. **Measure your waist** at the narrowest point of your torso, usually just above the belly button. Some guides use the navel instead. Pick one landmark and stick with it.
5. **Breathe out normally before measuring your waist.** Don't suck in, and don't push your stomach out.
6. **Take each measurement twice** and use the average if they differ.
7. **Measure under the same conditions each time**, ideally in the morning before eating, so day-to-day bloating doesn't distort your trend.

The measurement is most useful as a trend, not a one-off verdict. Recording it every few weeks tells you far more than a single reading.

## Where the 1.618 Golden Ratio Comes From

The golden ratio, roughly 1.618, is a mathematical proportion famous in art and architecture. Bodybuilding culture adopted it as the ideal V-taper.

The exact figure is best treated as a cultural convention, not a measured optimum. You may see calculator sites and fitness articles claim that women across cultures consistently pick 1.6 as the most attractive male shape. We could not verify that claim, and the recent research covered below does not support 1.618 specifically.

As one calculator site puts it, the golden ratio target is [an aesthetic convention, not a health standard](https://deltmax.app/shoulder-to-waist-ratio/).

## What the Research Actually Found

The evidence on shoulder and torso proportions is real, but more nuanced than the meme. Three findings matter most.

### Body Fat Outweighed the Ratio

One recent study looked at how body fat and shoulder to waist ratio relate to how attractive men's bodies are rated. Raters came from three populations. A [Psychology Today summary](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/finding-a-new-home/202507/what-makes-a-man-attractive-research-discovers-the-ideal-body) reports 283 participants from China, Lithuania and the UK.

According to the [study's published abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191886925002028?_bhlid=221f41b196ca84546a6c2cdc8b8de4fb3dc31ae8), attractiveness peaked at an SWR of **around 1.57**. Above that peak, the relationship was very flat and variable.

The more important finding came when ratio and body fat were analysed together. **Adiposity (body fat) explained more of the variation in attractiveness ratings than the ratio did**, and this held in all three populations. The Psychology Today summary also notes that waist size is closely tied to body fat levels.

The practical reading: a lean waist did more work than an extreme shoulder measurement.

### Both Waist and Shoulders Count

If body fat dominates, are shoulders irrelevant? No. A [University of Stirling manuscript on preferences for sexually dimorphic body traits](https://dspace.stir.ac.uk/bitstream/1893/30130/1/SPPS_20Manuscript_20Final.pdf) analysed several torso measures. Its models controlled for both waist and shoulder to waist ratio. A higher shoulder to waist ratio was still linked with attractiveness *beyond* the effect of waist size alone.

In plain terms, a narrow waist on its own and broad shoulders on their own each fall short of the two combined.

### Bigger Is Not Automatically Better

An EEG study (EEG records electrical activity in the brain) found a consistent pattern in its rating task. [Larger shoulder-to-hip ratios were rated more attractive than smaller ones](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37170034/), regardless of the sex of the figure or the rater.

Other work on muscularity complicates the "more is better" reading. A [paper testing why muscularity is rated attractive](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/6258161_Why_Is_Muscularity_Sexy_Tests_of_the_Fitness_Indicator_Hypothesis) found that **men with moderate muscularity were rated most attractive**. That fits the flat curve above 1.57 in the body-fat study. Past a point, extra shoulder size adds little.

Two more caveats are worth knowing:

- **These studies measure perceived attractiveness of images or avatars.** They say nothing direct about health, and they don't capture individual taste.
- **Image-based ratios may not match your tape number.** Studies typically compare body shapes on screen, while you measure circumference with a tape. Use research figures as rough reference points, not as a pass mark for your own tape reading.

## Shoulder to Waist Ratio for Women

Most online targets for this ratio are written for men. Women deserve a straight answer rather than a borrowed number.

There is [no single widely cited target ratio for women](https://bodilab.app/en/blog/shoulder-to-waist-ratio.html) the way 1.618 is cited for men. You may see figures such as "1.4 for women" on calculator sites. We could not find supporting data for them, so treat them as unverified. In the EEG study, raters judged larger shoulder-to-hip ratios as more attractive on female figures too. Brain activity, however, did not change with the ratio for female avatars.

For women interested in proportions, the more relevant shape is usually the overall silhouette rather than a single shoulder number. If you train shoulders and back for strength, posture or a style you like, that is reason enough. You don't need to chase a masculine V-taper benchmark. Measuring your ratio can still be a handy way to track training progress if you enjoy the data.

## How to Improve Your Ratio

A ratio has two levers. You can raise the top number or lower the bottom one. The findings above point to working on both, starting with the waist. The steps below are editorial training suggestions based on that reasoning, not measured outcomes.

### 1. Bring Your Waist Down First

Body fat explained more of the variation in attractiveness ratings than the ratio did. For most people, reducing waist circumference through fat loss is therefore the higher-value change.

- Run a modest, sustainable calorie deficit rather than a crash diet. Crash diets make it harder to keep the muscle you are trying to build.
- Keep protein intake consistent so you hold onto muscle while losing fat.
- Track your waist measurement alongside your body weight. The scale and the tape don't always move together.

Endless crunches won't target fat on your stomach specifically. Fat loss happens across the body.

### 2. Build the Muscles That Widen Your Frame

Shoulder circumference depends heavily on the **deltoids** (shoulder caps) and the **latissimus dorsi** ("lats"), the large back muscles that add width under the arms. Editorial training priorities:

- **Side delts:** lateral raises in moderate-to-higher rep ranges, done with control.
- **Overall shoulders:** overhead pressing with dumbbells or a barbell.
- **Lats and upper back:** pull-ups, lat pulldowns and rows.
- **Rear delts:** face pulls or reverse flyes for a fuller shoulder from every angle.

Aim to progress gradually, adding weight or reps over weeks. Moderate muscularity was rated more attractive than extreme size, so you don't need a competitive bodybuilder's build to improve your ratio.

### 3. Fix Your Posture

Rounded shoulders pull the shoulder line forward and narrow the look of your frame from the front. Rows, rear-delt work and attention to how you stand and sit all help you carry the width you already have. Posture changes how your shape reads to someone looking at you. It doesn't change the tape measurement, so keep your measuring routine consistent.

### 4. Dress for the Shape You Want

Clothing can change how your proportions read without changing your body. As editorial styling suggestions:

- **Structured shoulders** on jackets and blazers broaden the upper line.
- **A taper through the waist**, from tailored shirts or slim-cut jackets, sharpens the V.
- **Well-fitted t-shirts** that sit at the shoulder seam beat oversized cuts if you want to show a taper.
- **Vertical lines and darker colours around the midsection** draw less attention to the waist.

## Keep the Number in Perspective

Shoulder to waist ratio is a useful tool when you treat it as a progress tracker rather than a grade:

- **Measure consistently** or the number is noise.
- **Prioritise your waist.** In the multi-population study, body fat outweighed the ratio.
- **Build your shoulders and back** for a frame that tapers, without assuming bigger always looks better.
- **Ignore the decimal places.** The data reviewed here does not support 1.618 over 1.57, and attractiveness ratings were flat and variable above the peak anyway.
- **Remember what the studies measured:** ratings of images in specific groups of people, not your health or anyone's personal taste.

A smaller waist, stronger shoulders and upper back, decent posture and clothes that fit will move your ratio in the right direction.
