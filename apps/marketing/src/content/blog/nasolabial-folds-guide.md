You caught your face in a harsh bathroom light, or in a photo taken from slightly below, and two lines running from your nose to your mouth suddenly looked deeper than you remembered. Now you're searching for how to get rid of nasolabial folds.

Here's the useful version of that search. Nasolabial folds are a normal part of human anatomy. Everyone has them to some degree, and they deepen for structural reasons that a cream can't reach. Some factors are in your control, some aren't, and a lot of what you see depends on lighting and expression. This guide covers what the folds are, why they change, where the evidence stops, and the practical steps worth your time.

## What nasolabial folds actually are

Nasolabial folds are the creases that run from each side of your nose down toward the corners of your mouth. You'll also hear them called **smile lines** or **laugh lines**, as noted by the [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/23027-nasolabial-folds). Anatomists sometimes use the term **melolabial fold**, which describes the crease where the cheek (*melo*) meets the upper lip (*labial*).

That second name explains the feature better. The fold is a **boundary line** between two zones of soft tissue that behave differently:

- **Above and outside the line** is the cheek, with its own fat pads and looser support.
- **Below and inside the line** is the upper lip area, which is more firmly anchored to the muscles you use to smile and talk.

When those two zones shift relative to each other, the line between them gets more visible. That is most of the story.

## Why nasolabial folds deepen over time

The fold doesn't deepen mainly because the skin gets "wrinkled." Several layers change at once.

### The cheek mass moves

A long-running [photogrammetric study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8278486/) photographed the same people roughly every ten years from age 20 onward. With age, the cheek mass shifted **forward, outward and downward**, and the fold deepened as a result. The relationship between the upper lip and the fold itself stayed fairly constant. The authors concluded that deepening comes from changes in the cheek mass and its support, not from the lip area.

In plain terms, tissue above the line slides toward the line and drapes over it.

### Fat compartments change unevenly

An [anatomical review of why nasolabial folds appear](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11011544/) describes a two-sided change:

- The **superficial fat above the line** (called nasolabial fat) tends to enlarge with age.
- The **buccal fat below the line**, close to the fold, tends to shrink.
- As skin and supporting tissue above the line lose elasticity, they sag over the firmly anchored area below.

More tissue on one side, less on the other, and weaker support produce a deeper crease.

### Not every fold has the same cause

The same review sorts nasolabial folds into **three types** with different mechanisms. Two involve volume differences and age-related sagging. The third type is driven mainly by **fibers of the upper lip elevator muscles**, the muscles that lift your lip when you smile. Their names, like zygomaticus major and minor, come up often in anatomy texts.

That matters because the claim "smile lines have nothing to do with smiling" is too simple. For some people, muscle anatomy is a real part of why the line is visible, even at a young age.

### Skin quality: collagen, elastin, sun and smoking

Skin also contributes. Collagen and elastin give skin its ability to stretch and spring back. The Cleveland Clinic lists three main contributors to more prominent folds:

1. **Aging**, which flattens the cheekbones, lets gravity pull on looser skin, and reduces collagen and elastin.
2. **Smoking**, which destroys collagen and elastin.
3. **Sun damage**, since UV rays also break down collagen and elastin.

The same source notes that the creases become more prominent and permanent with age, especially in people who smoke or skip sun protection. Those two factors are the clearest levers you control.

## Are nasolabial folds a flaw?

No. A faint fold is normal anatomy for nearly every adult face. It shows up most when you smile, which is why it got the name *smile line*. A face with no fold at all during a full smile would look unusual.

What people usually dislike is a fold that reads as *tired* or *heavy* at rest. Even then, three things change how it looks from moment to moment:

- **Lighting.** Light from directly overhead casts shadows downward into any crease. A ceiling bulb in a bathroom is about the least flattering light for this area. *(Editorial observation, not a measured finding.)*
- **Camera angle.** A phone held low and close exaggerates the lower half of the face, including the folds.
- **Expression.** A held, tense smile bunches the cheek tissue toward the line. A relaxed expression spreads it out.

If your concern comes mostly from one bad photo or one mirror, check how the folds look in soft window light first. Many people find the problem is smaller than it seemed.

## How to get rid of nasolabial folds: what's realistic

You can't erase a structural crease with something applied to the surface. The main causes sit beneath the skin: fat compartments, the support of the cheek, and muscle attachments. A topical product works on the skin's surface layers, not on where the cheek mass sits.

What that means in practice:

- **No cream, serum or tool removes the fold.** We didn't find solid evidence that any topical product or facial device changes the fold itself. Treat "instant smile line eraser" marketing with skepticism.
- **Facial exercises, massage and gua sha** are popular online. We found no reliable evidence that they reduce nasolabial folds, so we don't recommend them for this purpose.
- **The realistic goals** are to avoid speeding up the process (sun and smoking), to keep your skin in good condition, and to manage how the area reads through grooming, styling, lighting and expression.

That isn't a consolation prize. These steps are low-risk, inexpensive, and they affect your whole face, not one line.

## Fillers and other procedures: our position

Search "nasolabial folds" and you'll quickly find dermal filler ads. Clinical trials on fillers for this area exist. One randomized, split-face trial of 45 people followed patients for nine months. But this site's position is clear: **We don't recommend fillers for appearance.** The same goes for thread lifts, facelifts and other injectables used to change how your face looks.

The risks are well documented:

- **Swelling and migration.** A [prospective study comparing filler types](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12743174/) notes that hyaluronic acid filler is frequently linked to transient swelling and possible movement of the product away from where it was placed.
- **Vascular complications.** Filler injected near blood vessels can block blood flow. A published case report describes a 34-year-old woman who developed **nasal skin necrosis** (death of skin tissue) after filler was injected into her nasolabial folds. The report also notes that the nasolabial folds and nose rank among the more common sites for this complication.
- **Temporary results.** Products break down, which leads to repeated procedures, repeated cost and repeated exposure to the same risks.

Thread lifts and surgery are invasive procedures with their own complications and recovery time. **Talk to a qualified professional before considering this** or any other procedure, and know that our recommendation is the non-medical approach below.

One exception has nothing to do with looks. If one side of your face **suddenly droops**, or the fold on one side flattens along with weakness or numbness, seek medical care right away. That is a medical issue, not a cosmetic one.

## Practical steps you control

These are editorial suggestions based on the causes above. They are not a treatment plan, and they won't change your underlying anatomy.

### 1. Protect your skin from the sun every day

Sun damage breaks down collagen and elastin, and unprotected sun exposure is linked to more prominent folds. Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen every morning, including on cloudy days. Shade and a brimmed hat count too.

### 2. Don't smoke, or quit if you do

Smoking destroys collagen and elastin. Of everything in this guide, quitting has the broadest benefit, well beyond your face.

### 3. Keep a simple, consistent skincare routine

A gentle cleanser, a moisturizer suited to your skin type and sunscreen cover the basics. The goal is healthy skin overall, not fold removal. Be wary of anything that promises to "fill" lines from a jar.

### 4. Use grooming and styling to change how the area reads

- **For beard wearers:** a well-kept beard or stubble with a clean cheek line shifts attention toward your jaw and away from the midface. Keep the mustache edge tidy so it doesn't sit in the crease and add a second line. *(Editorial styling suggestion.)*
- **For makeup wearers:** a light, hydrating base applied thinly sits better than heavy powder, which settles into creases. Some people add a soft, light-reflecting concealer along the upper edge of the line. *(Editorial styling suggestion.)*
- **Hair:** styles with some volume or framing around the cheeks balance the midface. Very flat, severe styles pulled tight from the face put more focus on it.

### 5. Manage lighting and angles in photos

- Face a window or use soft light at roughly eye level instead of a single overhead bulb.
- Hold the camera at or slightly above eye level, not below the chin.
- Relax your cheeks before smiling. A genuine, easy smile usually reads better than a held one.

## A quick reality check

Before you spend money or worry on this, run through these questions:

1. **Do the folds bother me in normal light, or only in harsh overhead light and low-angle photos?**
2. **Am I using daily sun protection and avoiding smoking?** If not, start there.
3. **Is my grooming or makeup working with my face or against it?**
4. **Am I comparing myself to filtered or edited images?** Many apps smooth this exact area by default.
5. **Is anything sudden or one-sided?** If yes, see a doctor for a medical check, not a cosmetic one.

## The bottom line

Nasolabial folds are normal anatomy. They deepen with age mainly because the cheek mass and its fat compartments shift and lose support, and for some people because of how the smile muscles attach. Sun exposure and smoking speed up the skin side of that process, and both are in your hands.

You can't scrub, massage or cream a structural fold away. You can protect your skin, groom and style with intent, choose better light, and keep the line in perspective as one small part of an expressive face.
