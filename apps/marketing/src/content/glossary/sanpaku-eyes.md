## What does sanpaku eyes mean

Sanpaku is Japanese for "three whites." The full term, sanpaku-gan (三白眼), translates literally to "three whites eyes" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/can-this-superstition-determine-your-fate-probably-not-5935597)). It describes eyes where the white (sclera) shows above or below the iris, on top of the usual white at either side ([All About Vision](https://www.allaboutvision.com/eye-care/cosmetic/sanpaku-eyes/)).

The usual split is yin sanpaku, with white beneath the iris, and yang sanpaku, with white above it ([Lens.com](https://www.lens.com/eyestyle/tips-advice/what-are-sanpaku-eyes/)). These labels are cultural, not clinical. Sanpaku is not a medical condition or diagnosis. It is also not the same as bulging eyes (proptosis), where the eyeball sits farther forward than usual ([Lens.com](https://www.lens.com/eyestyle/tips-advice/what-are-sanpaku-eyes/)).

Clinicians call the visible strip "scleral show." It often comes from natural eyelid and orbital anatomy, or from lids loosening with age ([Perfect Corp](https://www.perfectcorp.com/business/blog/general/scleral-show-ultimate-guide-2026)). One caveat matters here. Upper lid retraction with scleral show is the most common eye sign of thyroid eye disease ([Medscape](https://emedicine.medscape.com/article/1218444-overview)). If extra white appears suddenly, or comes with bulging, redness, dryness or double vision, see an eye doctor.

## Origin

The idea spread in the West through George Ohsawa, a Japanese macrobiotic author. His writings were published in English as *You Are All Sanpaku* ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Ohsawa)). He framed sanpaku as a sign that a person's whole system was out of balance. He also claimed he had foreseen danger for President Kennedy before the assassination ([Vision Center](https://www.visioncenter.org/blog/sanpaku-eyes/)). He tied yin sanpaku to self-destructive behavior and yang sanpaku to rage ([Grunge](https://www.grunge.com/1226405/sanpaku-eyes-the-japanese-superstition-explained/)). None of this has scientific support, and several people he flagged lived long lives ([Vision Center](https://www.visioncenter.org/blog/sanpaku-eyes/)). It also doesn't appear to be a major superstition in Japan itself ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/can-this-superstition-determine-your-fate-probably-not-5935597)). Manga does use sanpaku eyes to make characters look threatening ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanpaku)).

## How it relates to looksmaxxing

Online, sanpaku eyes is meme slang. People use it to "predict" fates or read personality from photos, often with a diagram of example eyes ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/sanpaku-eyes)). In looksmaxxing threads it comes up in eye-area debates. Some call it an intense stare and others call it a flaw, but both are opinions. It says nothing about character.

Editorial suggestion: in our observation, raised brows, a wide-eyed expression or a tilted head can all show more white in a photo. When comparing your eye area across pictures, shoot at eye level with a relaxed face. Eye tilt shifts with head pose and camera roll in the same way.
