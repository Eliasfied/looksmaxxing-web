## What does caging mean

Caging refers to a period of strict caloric restriction aimed primarily at dropping facial fat. The goal is to lower overall body fat to a point where the face looks leaner, more angular, and more defined. Users typically target features like cheekbones, the jawline, and under-eye hollowing, all of which become more visible as subcutaneous fat decreases.

The term is used loosely in looksmaxxing spaces. Some people use it to describe any aggressive cut, while others reserve it for extreme deficits. In practice, caging usually means eating well below maintenance calories for weeks or months. This is distinct from a standard diet because the explicit motivation is facial aesthetics rather than general health or athletic performance.

Results vary depending on starting body fat, genetics, and bone structure. Some users report noticeable improvements in jawline definition and midface sharpness after a sustained cut. Research suggests that facial adiposity, the amount of fat stored in the face, is a real factor in how angular or soft someone's features appear.

## Origin

The term draws on the name of actor Nicolas Cage, who is known for having a lean, angular, and highly defined facial structure. In online looksmaxxing communities, his face became shorthand for the aesthetic outcome of aggressive cutting. "Caging" evolved as a verb from that association.

## How it relates to looksmaxxing

Caging is considered one of the more accessible looksmaxxing methods because it requires no procedures or supplements. Reducing body fat is free and achievable through diet alone, which makes it a common starting point for people new to looksmaxxing.

That said, aggressive caloric restriction carries real health risks including muscle loss, fatigue, hormonal disruption, and nutrient deficiencies. Dropping body fat too fast can also age the face by reducing volume in areas where some fullness is actually desirable. If you use the Aura app to track your jawline score or facial sharpness over time, a moderate, sustained cut tends to show better results than a crash approach. A sustainable deficit with adequate protein is generally more effective than extreme restriction.
