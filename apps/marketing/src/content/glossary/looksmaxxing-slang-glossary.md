## What does hagmaxxing mean

Hagmaxxing refers to intentionally leaning into facial features that read as sharp, hollow, or severe. Think pronounced cheekbones, deep-set eyes, a strong jawline with visible muscle striations, and low body fat that pulls the face tight. Rather than softening these traits, someone who is hagmaxxing treats them as assets.

The term sits somewhere between ironic and sincere. Some people use it to describe a genuine aesthetic goal, particularly men who score well on PSL metrics for sharpness and angularity but feel their look is "too harsh" by mainstream standards. Others use it humorously to describe any looksmaxxing approach that prioritizes intimidating or striking features over approachability. Either way, the core idea is the same: stop fighting the sharp structure and start building around it.

Practically, hagmaxxing often involves maximizing facial leanness through low body fat, skipping any grooming choices that soften features (like rounded haircuts or full beards that obscure the jawline), and sometimes incorporating dark or minimal styling to reinforce the aesthetic.

## Origin

The term comes from online looksmaxxing communities, blending "hag" (an old word for a gaunt or harsh-looking older woman) with the standard "-maxxing" suffix used to describe optimizing a specific trait. It was coined partly as a joke but got picked up as a genuine label for a recognizable aesthetic category.

In PSL and blackpill rating frameworks, facial sharpness and angularity are generally scored positively for men.
