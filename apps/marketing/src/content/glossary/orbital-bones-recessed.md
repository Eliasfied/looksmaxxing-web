## What does recessed infras mean

Recessed infras is shorthand for recessed infraorbital rims, the bony ridges that run directly beneath the eyes. When these bones sit further back relative to the cornea and cheekbones, the under-eye area appears hollow, the cheeks look flat, and the overall midface loses definition. The opposite, well-projected infraorbitals, creates a fuller, more supported look under the eyes and contributes to what many consider a strong midface.

The degree of recession is often assessed by checking whether the lower eyelid rim sits behind or in front of the cornea when viewed from the side. Significant recession can also make dark circles and tear troughs appear more pronounced, since there is less bony support pushing soft tissue forward. This is distinct from orbital bone depth, which relates to how deep-set the eyes are within the skull.

## Origin

The term gained traction in online aesthetics communities that applied clinical anatomy vocabulary to everyday facial analysis. "Infraorbital" comes directly from medical terminology, describing the region inferior to the orbit. Shortened to "infras" for convenience, the phrase became a standard part of looksmaxxing vocabulary when discussions around midface projection and facial support structures became more detailed.

## How it relates to looksmaxxing

Infraorbital projection is considered one of the more significant midface traits in PSL-style facial assessment. Those working on looksmaxxing often flag recessed infras as a factor behind tired-looking eyes or weak midface scores. Non-surgical approaches such as mewing, face yoga, or targeted facial exercises may help with soft-tissue support over time, though their effect on bone structure in adults is debated and research is limited.

Orbital rim implants and under-eye filler are also sold for this.
